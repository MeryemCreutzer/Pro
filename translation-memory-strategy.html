Translation Memory & Terminology Strategy

A localization quality project focused on cleaning polluted translation memories and building a scalable terminology strategy in Crowdin.

Translation quality improves when linguistic resources are clean, trusted, and structured.

This project addressed the quality of the translation memories and terminology resources that translators, reviewers, and machine translation workflows depend on.

PcVue
Role

Platform
Crowdin
Published
May 3, 2026

Context

As part of a localization improvement initiative, I transformed an external consultancy report into a concrete, actionable workflow for managing linguistic resources in Crowdin.

The objective was to address a critical quality issue: heavily polluted translation memories affecting translation quality, consistency, and efficiency across product documentation.

Tools & Technologies

  • Crowdin
  • DeepL
  • TMX processing and scripting approaches
  • Glossary and terminology management systems

Polluted TMs

  • Duplicate entries
  • Incorrect or misaligned translations
  • Noise from XML and MadCap tags
  • Legacy and unused segments

Tool Limitations

  • No regex support for bulk cleanup in native TM management
  • Limited batch editing capabilities
  • Limited validation filtering

Terminology Gaps

  • No structured glossary strategy
  • Inconsistent product-specific terms
  • No alignment between UI terminology and documentation

Progressive Workflow

01

Stabilization

Back up the TM with TMX export, remove duplicates, and delete low-value entries such as XML declarations, MadCap tags, unused entries, and old segments.

02

Quality Improvement

Adjust TM settings, introduce context-based matching, review parser configuration, and allow controlled on-the-fly corrections during translation.

03

Structural Cleanup

Evaluate rebuilding the TM from clean project data or processing TMX externally with scripts and conditional cleanup rules.

04

Terminology Strategy

Create structured Crowdin glossaries, integrate product terminology, standardize preferred and forbidden terms, and prepare DeepL integration.

My Role

  • Analyzed a technical report from translation experts
  • Translated recommendations into an operational workflow
  • Defined cleanup actions and implementation steps
  • Designed a scalable terminology management approach

Key Insight

Translation quality issues are rarely caused by translators alone. They are often caused by poorly maintained linguistic resources.

By cleaning and structuring translation memories and glossaries, translation becomes more consistent, QA becomes more reliable, machine translation improves, and overall localization effort decreases.

Outcome

The work resulted in a clear step-by-step cleanup methodology, a scalable terminology framework, and a foundation for AI-assisted translation workflows.

It also positioned the localization process for future improvements, including LLM-based translation and QA workflows within Crowdin.

What This Demonstrates

  • Turning complex reports into actionable workflows
  • Understanding localization systems and linguistic resources
  • Improving quality, consistency, and scalability
  • Owning end-to-end process improvement
